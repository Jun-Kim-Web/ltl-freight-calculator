Freight Class Guide & Resources

What is LTL Freight Class?

LTL (Less Than Truckload) Freight Class is a standardized classification system used by carriers to categorize shipments based on their density, handling requirements, stowability, and liability. The National Motor Freight Traffic Association (NMFTA) establishes these classifications through the National Motor Freight Classification (NMFC) standards.

Freight classes range from 50 to 500, with lower numbers indicating higher density and easier-to-handle shipments. Class 50 represents the densest, most compact freight (like bricks or steel plates), while Class 500 is reserved for items with very low density that require special handling.

Understanding your freight class is essential because it directly impacts shipping costs. Carriers use freight class to determine pricing, as higher classes (lower density) typically cost more to ship due to the space they occupy relative to their weight.

How to Calculate Density for Shipping?

Density is the key factor in determining freight class. It represents how much weight occupies a given volume. The formula for calculating density is straightforward:

Density (lbs/ft³) = Weight (lbs) ÷ Volume (ft³)

To calculate volume, multiply the length, width, and height of your shipment in inches, then divide by 1,728 (the number of cubic inches in a cubic foot):

Volume (ft³) = (Length × Width × Height in inches) ÷ 1,728

Example: If your shipment measures 48" × 40" × 36" and weighs 500 lbs:

  • Volume = (48 × 40 × 36) ÷ 1,728 = 69,120 ÷ 1,728 = 40 ft³
  • Density = 500 ÷ 40 = 12.5 lbs/ft³
  • This density falls in the Class 85 range (12-13.5 lbs/ft³)

List of NMFC Codes

The NMFC (National Motor Freight Classification) system uses numeric codes to classify freight. While there are thousands of specific NMFC codes for different commodities, freight classes are grouped into standard density ranges. Here are some common examples:

Freight Class Density Range (lbs/ft³) Common Examples
Class 50 ≥ 50 Bricks, steel plates, concrete blocks
Class 55 35 - 50 Machinery parts, castings
Class 60 30 - 35 Car parts, auto accessories
Class 70 15 - 22.5 Carpet rolls, tires
Class 85 12 - 13.5 Cast iron stoves, appliances
Class 100 9 - 10.5 Furniture, cabinets
Class 125 7 - 8 Wine cases, small appliances
Class 150 6 - 7 Auto body parts, bookcases
Class 200 4 - 5 Lampshades, mattresses
Class 250 3 - 4 Fish tanks, bicycles
Class 300 2 - 3 Empty barrels, large boxes
Class 400 1 - 2 Ping pong balls, empty containers

Note: Specific NMFC codes may override density-based classification for certain commodities. Always consult with your carrier for the most accurate classification.

Tips for Reducing Shipping Costs

Understanding freight class and optimizing your shipments can significantly reduce shipping costs. Here are practical tips to help you save money:

  1. Improve Packaging Efficiency: Use compact, space-efficient packaging. Reducing the overall volume of your shipment while maintaining weight can lower your freight class and shipping costs.
  2. Consolidate Shipments: Combine multiple smaller shipments into one larger shipment when possible. This can improve density and reduce per-unit shipping costs.
  3. Accurate Measurements: Always provide precise dimensions and weight. Incorrect measurements can result in reclassification and additional charges.
  4. Choose the Right Packaging: Use packaging that protects your items without adding unnecessary bulk. Consider custom-sized boxes that fit your products snugly.
  5. Understand Density Impact: Aim for higher density (lower freight class) when possible. Even small improvements in density can move your shipment to a lower class and reduce costs.
  6. Work with Reliable Carriers: Build relationships with carriers who understand your shipping needs and can provide accurate classifications.
  7. Review Classifications Regularly: As your products or packaging change, recalculate freight classes to ensure you're getting the best rates.
  8. Consider Pallet Optimization: Properly stacking and securing items on pallets can maximize space utilization and improve density.
